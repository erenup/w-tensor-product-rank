\section{From Gram invertibility to a rank lower bound}\label{sec:rank}

The matrix $\cG$ is the weighted bilinear Gram matrix of the evaluation
vectors. Its invertibility gives the required lower bound on the
number of decomposition terms.

\begin{lemma}[The dimension bound]\label{lem:dimension}
For the matrices in \Cref{lem:gram},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dimension}
\cG\text{ invertible}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \rank E=M
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r\ge M.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $Ec=0$, then $\cG c=E^{\mathsf T}WEc=0$.
Invertibility of $\cG$ gives $c=0$, so the $M$ columns of $E$ are
linearly independent in $\C^r$.
\end{proof}

This implication requires neither a positive weight matrix nor a
Hermitian inner product. It remains to choose the $M$ test functions
and prove that their Gram matrix is invertible for at least one
admissible finite parameter vector $t$.
