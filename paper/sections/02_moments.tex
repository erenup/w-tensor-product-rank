\section{Coefficient matching and moment conditions}\label{sec:moments}

\begin{lemma}[Moment conditions]\label{lem:moments}
The normalized identity \eqref{eq:normalized} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
\sum_{\ell=1}^r w_\ell\prod_{i=1}^k z_{i\ell}^{e_i}
=\prod_{i=1}^k m_i(e_i),
\qquad 0\le e_i\le d_i,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mi}
m_i(e)=
\begin{cases}
\gamma_i,&e=0,\\
\beta_i:=1/d_i,&e=1,\\
0,&2\le e\le d_i.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Compare the coefficients of $\prod_iX_i^{d_i-e_i}Y_i^{e_i}$ on both
sides of \eqref{eq:normalized} and divide both by
$\prod_i\binom{d_i}{e_i}$. On the right, the factor
$(X_i+z_{i\ell}Y_i)^{d_i}$ contributes $\binom{d_i}{e_i}z_{i\ell}^{e_i}$.
The coefficient contributed by the $i$th factor on the left is
$\gamma_i$ when $e_i=0$, is $1$ when $e_i=1$, and is zero otherwise;
after division by $\binom{d_i}{e_i}$ these are $\gamma_i$,
$\beta_i=1/d_i$ and $0$.
This gives \eqref{eq:moments}--\eqref{eq:mi}.
Conversely, these identities match every coefficient of the two
multihomogeneous polynomials, so they imply \eqref{eq:normalized}.
\end{proof}

The term \emph{moment} refers to the weighted monomial evaluations on
the left of \eqref{eq:moments}. The weights are complex; they are not
assumed positive or normalized to sum to one. In particular, the
moment conditions retain all the information in the normalized
polynomial identity, rather than providing only necessary tests.
