\section{A change of coordinates}\label{sec:coordinates}

Fix any decomposition \eqref{eq:decomposition} and delete its zero
summands; this does not increase $r$.
We first replace each pair of coefficients $(a_{i\ell},b_{i\ell})$ by one
affine parameter, absorbing its scale into the weight of the summand.

\begin{lemma}[Affine normalization]\label{lem:coordinates}
There exist $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_k\in\C$ such that the invertible
coordinate changes $x_i=X_i$, $y_i=\gamma_iX_i+Y_i$ transform
\eqref{eq:decomposition} into the equivalent identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized}
\prod_{i=1}^k X_i^{d_i-1}(\gamma_iX_i+Y_i)
=\sum_{\ell=1}^r w_\ell\prod_{i=1}^k(X_i+z_{i\ell}Y_i)^{d_i},
\end{equation}
where every $w_\ell$ is nonzero and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zw}
z_{i\ell}=\frac{b_{i\ell}}{a_{i\ell}+\gamma_i b_{i\ell}},
\qquad
w_\ell=c_\ell\prod_{i=1}^k(a_{i\ell}+\gamma_i b_{i\ell})^{d_i}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each $i$, choose $\gamma_i$ so that
$a_{i\ell}+\gamma_i b_{i\ell}\ne0$ for every $\ell$. Because zero
summands were deleted, every $c_\ell\ne0$ and every pair
$(a_{i\ell},b_{i\ell})\ne(0,0)$. Such a choice is therefore
possible: a pair with $b_{i\ell}\ne0$ excludes only
$-a_{i\ell}/b_{i\ell}$, and a pair with $b_{i\ell}=0$ has
$a_{i\ell}\ne0$ and excludes nothing.
After the coordinate change,
$a_{i\ell}x_i+b_{i\ell}y_i
=(a_{i\ell}+\gamma_i b_{i\ell})(X_i+z_{i\ell}Y_i)$.
Substitution gives \eqref{eq:normalized} and \eqref{eq:zw}; hence
every $w_\ell\ne0$.
\end{proof}

Arrange the parameters into the matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Zc}
Z_c=
\begin{pmatrix}
z_{11}&z_{12}&\cdots&z_{1r}\\
z_{21}&z_{22}&\cdots&z_{2r}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
z_{k1}&z_{k2}&\cdots&z_{kr}
\end{pmatrix}\in\C^{k\times r},
\qquad z_\ell=(z_{1\ell},\ldots,z_{k\ell})\in\C^k.
\end{equation}
Its $\ell$th column specifies the point $z_\ell$. Thus the original
decomposition determines $r$ points with weights $w_\ell$.
These points are fixed in the subsequent argument; we choose functions
to evaluate at them. Repeated points and zero coordinates are allowed.
The symbol $Z=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k)$ will denote formal variables, distinct
from the parameter matrix $Z_c$.
