\begin{abstract}
The known upper bound for products of generalized W tensors suggests a
matching lower bound on every decomposition with the symmetric blocks
fixed. We obtain it by normalizing coordinates, matching coefficients as
moment conditions, and constructing a weighted bilinear Gram matrix of
explicit test functions. Invertibility of this matrix forces the number
of summands to be at least the number of test functions. Exact Laurent
coefficients prove nondegeneracy in one finite choice of parameters,
including for decompositions with repeated nodes and zero coordinates.
More generally, for every $k\geq1$ and integers $a_i\geq b_i\geq1$, we prove
that the complex partially symmetric rank is
\[
 \Rk\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^k x_i^{a_i}y_i^{b_i}\right)
 =\prod_{i=1}^k(b_i+1)
   +\sum_{i=1}^k(a_i-b_i)\prod_{j\ne i}(b_j+1).
\]
A finite-character construction supplies the matching upper bound.
For generalized W tensors of orders $d_i\geq3$, the formula becomes
$2^{k-1}(\sum_i d_i-2k+2)$, attaining the upper bound of
Canino--Casarotti--Santarsiero. Three cubic W tensors have rank $20$,
and three copies of $x^3y^2$ have rank $54$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Rank, the main result, and its context}\label{sec:intro}

For $k\geq2$ and $d_i\geq3$, Canino--Casarotti--Santarsiero's
construction gives the upper bound
$M=2^{k-1}(\sum_i d_i-2k+2)$ for the fixed-block partially symmetric
rank of $\prod_i x_i^{d_i-1}y_i$ \cite[Theorem~1.1]{CCS}.
To prove the matching lower bound, discard zero summands from any
proposed decomposition and let $r$ count the remaining terms.
An invertible change of coordinates makes its factors affine, with their
scales absorbed into nonzero weights. Matching every coefficient gives
product-moment identities. By linearity these determine weighted
evaluation on the polynomial space with coordinate degrees at most $d_i$.

The proof constructs $M$ test functions in batches $\mathcal B_0,
\mathcal B_1,\ldots,\mathcal B_k$, whose evaluations form an $r\times M$
matrix $E$. With an auxiliary diagonal weight matrix $W$, their Gram
matrix is $G=E^{\mathsf T}WE$. Once $G$ is invertible, $E$ has independent
columns and $r\geq M$. The main step is to make every diagonal Gram block
nondegenerate at one finite parameter vector; orthogonality alone is
insufficient. The product-moment proof below establishes this step in
the broader binary-monomial setting and retains its full parameter range.


Let $k\geq1$, let $d_1,\ldots,d_k$ be positive integers, and identify
\[
 \bigotimes_{i=1}^k\Sym^{d_i}(\C^2)
\]
with the polynomials in disjoint pairs $(x_i,y_i)$ that have degree $d_i$
in the $i$th pair. For a nonzero such polynomial $F$, its fixed-block
partially symmetric rank $\Rk_{\mathbf d}(F)$ is the least $n$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-definition}
 F=\sum_{\ell=1}^n c_\ell
        \prod_{i=1}^k (u_{i,\ell}x_i+v_{i,\ell}y_i)^{d_i},
 \qquad c_\ell,u_{i,\ell},v_{i,\ell}\in\C.
\end{equation}
The degrees and the $k$ blocks remain fixed. Coefficients may be absorbed
into a factor by extracting a complex $d_i$th root, so this definition
agrees with the definition using sums of partially symmetric pure
tensors without displayed scalar coefficients. Multiplication of $F$
by a nonzero scalar also leaves its rank unchanged. We suppress the
subscript $\mathbf d$ when the degrees are clear.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every integer $k\geq1$ and integers $a_i\geq b_i\geq1$, set
$d_i=a_i+b_i$. Then, over $\C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-rank}
 \Rk_{\mathbf d}\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^k x_i^{a_i}y_i^{b_i}\right)
 =N(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)
 :=\prod_{i=1}^k(b_i+1)
   +\sum_{i=1}^k(a_i-b_i)\prod_{j\ne i}(b_j+1).
\end{equation}
Empty products have value $1$. The lower bound holds for every finite
decomposition of the form \eqref{eq:rank-definition}, without restrictions
on the complex coefficients or support. The upper bound is realized by
the explicit finite-character identity \eqref{eq:character-decomposition}.
\end{theorem}

The theorem concerns exact complex rank with fixed symmetric blocks.
No assertion about real rank, border rank, cactus rank, unrestricted
nonsymmetric rank, or rank after grouping modes from different blocks
is included. The lower argument uses only finite decompositions and
does not apply to limits of decompositions.

For $d\geq3$, the generalized W tensor is
\[
 W_d=\sum_{j=1}^d e_1^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes e_2
                         \otimes e_1^{\otimes(d-j)}
       \in\Sym^d(\C^2).
\]
Under the usual polynomial identification this is a nonzero scalar
multiple of $x^{d-1}y$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:W}
For every $k\geq2$ and integers $d_i\geq3$, the partially symmetric
rank of the product of generalized W tensors is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W-rank}
 \Rk_{\mathbf d}(W_{d_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes W_{d_k})
   =2^{k-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^k d_i-2k+2\right).
\end{equation}
In particular $\Rk_{(3,3,3)}(W_3^{\otimes3})=20$ and
$\Rk_{(3,\ldots,3)}(W_3^{\otimes k})=2^{k-1}(k+2)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $a_i=d_i-1$ and $b_i=1$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and use
invariance of rank under nonzero scalar multiplication.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Related decomposition questions.}
SOS tensor decompositions write a real homogeneous polynomial of even
degree as a sum of squares of real homogeneous polynomials of half that
degree; SOS-rank counts the smallest number of those squares
\cite[Section~4]{CLQ}. Here the admissible terms are products of powers of complex linear forms
in prescribed symmetric blocks. These are different decomposition
problems, and their ranks are not identified. No SOS decomposition theorem
is used in either bound.


\subsection{The two constructions}

The lower bound will be proved first for arbitrary finite product-moment
formulas. The moments vanish above a prescribed degree $b_i$ in each
coordinate, while the moment at degree $b_i$ is nonzero. There are
$Q=\prod_i(b_i+1)$ basic evaluation vectors. For each coordinate an
additional list contributes $(d_i-2b_i)Q/(b_i+1)$ vectors. In the
original coordinates the corresponding Gram matrices can be singular,
so orthogonality alone does not justify adding dimensions.

We introduce parameters $t_i$ and replace the diagonal pairing weights
$w_\ell$ by $w_\ell\prod_i(1+t_i z_{i,\ell})^{d_i}$. Suitable
rational evaluation vectors remain orthogonal in distinct lists.
Within each additional list, reversing block columns gives a triangular
Gram matrix. Its diagonal block has an invertible leading Laurent
coefficient. Each determinant is therefore a nonzero rational function,
and one finite parameter vector avoids all its zeros and poles.

For the upper bound, write $q_i=b_i+1$ and $\delta_i=a_i-b_i$.
The column lattice of
$A=\diag(q_1,\ldots,q_k)+\delta\mathbf1^T$ has index $N$ and meets
$\prod_i[-a_i,b_i]$ only at the origin. Summing over the characters
of $\Z^k/A\Z^k$ then selects exactly the desired monomial among all
coefficients of a sum of partially symmetric powers. Both bounds are
proved here; the tensor-rank results cited for context are not used
as mathematical premises.

\section{From decompositions to product moments}\label{sec:reduction}

We now deduce the lower bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from
Theorem~\ref{thm:moment}. This reduction begins with an arbitrary
decomposition; no affine-support hypothesis is imposed on it.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, every decomposition
of $F=\prod_i x_i^{a_i}y_i^{b_i}$ has at least
$N(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)$ summands.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a decomposition \eqref{eq:rank-definition} of length $n$ and delete
any zero summands. Thus every $c_\ell$ and every pair
$(u_{i,\ell},v_{i,\ell})$ is nonzero. For each $i$, choose
$\gamma_i\in\C$ so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-chart}
 u_{i,\ell}+\gamma_i v_{i,\ell}\ne0
                    \qquad(1\leq\ell\leq n).
\end{equation}
If $v_{i,\ell}=0$, this condition is automatic; otherwise it excludes
one value of $\gamma_i$. Only finitely many values are excluded, so
the choice exists. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:node-weight}
 z_{i,\ell}=\frac{v_{i,\ell}}{u_{i,\ell}+\gamma_i v_{i,\ell}},
 \qquad
 w_\ell=c_\ell\prod_{i=1}^k
                 (u_{i,\ell}+\gamma_i v_{i,\ell})^{d_i}.
\end{equation}
All weights are nonzero. Nodes may repeat, and $z_{i,\ell}$ is allowed
to be zero.

For $0\leq e_i\leq d_i$, apply the differential operator
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^k
 (\partial_{x_i}+\gamma_i\partial_{y_i})^{d_i-e_i}
 \partial_{y_i}^{e_i}
\]
to the decomposition and divide by $\prod_i d_i!$. On its $\ell$th
summand the result is $w_\ell\prod_i z_{i,\ell}^{e_i}$.
On the target monomial the result factors over the blocks. The
one-variable functionals obtained in this way satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:monomial-moments}
 m_i(z_i^e)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{(d_i-e)!\,b_i!}{(b_i-e)!\,d_i!}
                  \gamma_i^{b_i-e},&0\leq e\leq b_i,\\[6pt]
 0,&b_i<e\leq d_i.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Indeed, when $e\leq b_i$, differentiation by $\partial_{y_i}^e$
first contributes $b_i!/(b_i-e)!$. In the remaining binomial expansion
of $(\partial_{x_i}+\gamma_i\partial_{y_i})^{d_i-e}$, only the
term with $b_i-e$ derivatives in $y_i$ and $a_i$ derivatives in
$x_i$ survives. Its value gives \eqref{eq:monomial-moments}.
For $e>b_i$ the first differentiation already vanishes.

Consequently the product-moment identities hold throughout the entire
box $0\leq e_i\leq d_i$, and
\[
 \beta_i=m_i(z_i^{b_i})
         =\frac{a_i!\,b_i!}{d_i!}
         =\binom{d_i}{b_i}^{-1}\ne0.
\]
Because $a_i\geq b_i$, we have $d_i\geq2b_i$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:moment} gives
\[
 n\geq\prod_i(b_i+1)
       +\sum_i(d_i-2b_i)\prod_{j\ne i}(b_j+1)
   =N(\mathbf a,\mathbf b),
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

The initial chart \eqref{eq:initial-chart} and the auxiliary parameters
in the moment proof serve different purposes. The first makes an
arbitrary finite decomposition affine. The second makes the Gram
blocks nondegenerate. Both choices are made after fixing the
decomposition, and both follow from explicit finite nonvanishing
conditions. Neither is a genericity assumption on the decomposition.

Proposition~\ref{prop:lower} bounds every decomposition below by

\section{Specializations and scope}\label{sec:specializations}

For one block, \eqref{eq:main-rank} is $\Rk(x^ay^b)=a+1$ when
$a\geq b\geq1$. For two blocks it becomes
\begin{align*}
 N&=(b_1+1)(b_2+1)+(a_1-b_1)(b_2+1)+(a_2-b_2)(b_1+1)\\
  &=(a_1+1)(a_2+1)-(a_1-b_1)(a_2-b_2).
\end{align*}
These are the one-block and two-block overlaps described above.
When all $a_i=b_i$, the additional moment lists are absent and the
formula reduces to $N=\prod_i(b_i+1)$; the lattice matrix in the
upper construction is then diagonal.

Some immediate examples are recorded in Table~\ref{tab:examples}.
They are consequences of the quantified theorem, not an interpolation
from finitely many rank computations.
\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Fixed-block tensor or polynomial & Complex partially symmetric rank\\
\midrule
$x^ay^b$, $a\geq b\geq1$ & $a+1$\\
$\prod_i x_i^{b_i}y_i^{b_i}$ & $\prod_i(b_i+1)$\\
$W_3^{\otimes k}$, $k\geq2$ & $2^{k-1}(k+2)$\\
$W_3\otimes W_3\otimes W_3$ & $20$\\
$(x^3y^2)^{\otimes3}$ & $54$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Special cases of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Each tensor power
keeps its displayed factors as symmetric blocks.}\label{tab:examples}
\end{table}

For the last example $q_i=3$ and $\delta_i=1$, so
$N=3^3+3\cdot3^2=54$. Its lattice matrix has diagonal entries $4$
and off-diagonal entries $1$, and \eqref{eq:character-decomposition}
provides the $54$ summands. For the cubic W triple the corresponding
matrix has diagonal entries $3$ and off-diagonal entries $1$, and
the number of characters is $20$.

The hypothesis $b_i\geq1$ is essential to the theorem as stated.
It is used when the Laurent remainder is evaluated at a zero node.
The formula would already be false for the single pure power $x^a$
with $b=0$: its rank is $1$, whereas the right-hand side would be
$a+1$ for $a\geq1$. Such factors are excluded from the statement.

All Gram forms in the proof are symmetric bilinear forms over $\C$.
No positive weights or Hermitian positivity are assumed. Invertibility
of every diagonal Gram block, established before dimensions are added,
is what prevents isotropic or radical subspaces from invalidating the
count. The Laurent expansions certify nonzero rational functions;
the final argument evaluates those functions at one finite point.
No limiting decomposition enters the proof.

\section{Exact finite checks}\label{app:checks}

The proof above establishes the theorem for all its stated parameters
without a computer calculation. Two finite sets of exact checks provide
additional arithmetic checks on its formulas. Their scope is described
here to distinguish them from the universal proof.

For the W-state specialization, rational calculations in degrees $2$
through $9$ check the univariate moment and Gram identities. A
calculation over $\Q(\zeta_{10})$ verifies all coefficients of a
$20$-term cubic triple decomposition and an invertible $20\times20$
Gram matrix at an asymmetric finite parameter vector. Rejection
checks change a coefficient, choose a pole, and impose an incorrect
nondegeneracy assertion at a symmetric chart. The last check rejects
that chart, while leaving the validity of the decomposition intact.

For the general binary-monomial construction, integer, rational, and
cyclotomic arithmetic verifies all coefficients in eight explicit
character decompositions, including the $54$-term example, unequal
degrees, a balanced factor, four blocks, and the one-block, two-block,
and cubic-W overlaps. Three rational cubatures check their full Gram
matrices directly, with zero and repeated nodes included. Separate
identities check the Laurent remainder at positive, negative, and
zero rational nodes. Eight rejection checks alter, respectively, the
rank count, lattice orientation, character list, character value,
target phase, cubature weight, Gram-vector list, and remainder sign.

These finite computations use no floating-point rank tests. They
cannot replace the finite-chart existence proof, the all-parameter
lattice argument, or the reduction of an arbitrary decomposition to
moments. The mathematical conclusions do not rely on an assumption
that the checked examples exhaust the possible supports.
